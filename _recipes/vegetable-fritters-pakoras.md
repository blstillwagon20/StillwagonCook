> Pakoras are popular spiced, batter-dipped, deep-fried vegetables that make perfect snacks or appetizers. Serve hot pakoras with your favorite chutney or dip.

Try batter frying various types of vegetables. Cauliflower pakoras are probably the most popular, but equally delicious are potato rings, mushrooms, zucchini chunks, spinach leaves, pumpkin slices, eggplant rings, baby tomatoes, sweet potatoes, red or green pepper slices, asparagus tips, and artichoke hearts. Cook pakoras slowly to ensure that the batter and the vegetables cook simultaneously. You needn't precook the vegetables.

These are the best-tasting fried vegetables I've ever had, though one of the ingredients is hard to find; it's the chickpea flour. You can usually buy it at an herbal health food store or they can probably order it for you. But it's well worth the hassle. You could try using regular flour for all of it; it would probably be good also, though I haven't tried it myself.

## Ingredients

- ⅔ cup chickpea flour
- ⅔ cup plain flour
- ⅔ cup self-raising flour
- 2½ tsp. salt
- 2 tsp. garlic powder
- 1½ tsp. turmeric
- ½ tsp. cayenne pepper
- 1½ tsp. ground coriander
- 2 tsp. green chilies, seeded and finely chopped
- About 2½ cups cold water, or enough to make a smooth batter
- Bite-sized vegetable pieces of your choice
- Ghee or oil for deep-frying

## Steps

1. Combine the flours (⅔ cup), salt (2½ tsp.), powdered spices, and green chilies (2 tsp.) in a bowl. Mix well with a wire whisk.
2. Slowly add cold water while whisking the batter until it achieves the consistency of medium-light cream. When you dip the vegetable in the batter, it should be completely coated but neither thick and heavy nor runny and thin. Have extra flour and water on hand to adjust the consistency as required. Let the batter sit for 10 to 15 minutes.
3. Heat fresh ghee or oil, to the depth of 2½ to 3 inches, in a wok or deep-fryer until the temperature reaches about 355°F. Dip 5 or 6 pieces of vegetable in the batter and, one at a time, carefully slip them into the hot oil.
4. The temperature will fall, but try to maintain it between 345°F and 355°F throughout the frying. Fry until the pakoras are golden brown, turning to cook them evenly on all sides.
5. Remove with a slotted spoon and drain on paper towels. Continue cooking until all the pakoras are done.
6. Serve immediately or keep warm, uncovered, in a preheated cool oven for up to ½ hour.
